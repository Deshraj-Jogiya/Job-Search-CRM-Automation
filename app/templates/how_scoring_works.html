Two numbers, two questions

Fit answers "does this resume match this job". Opportunity answers "is this posting worth the effort": sponsorship record, pay level, worksite and freshness. They are shown apart so a strong sponsor can never hide a poor fit. The queue sorts by a combined total of both.

Fit

The rule-based fit is computed from the posting and the profile alone, with no AI and no network call:

Skill overlap60%Technical requirements named in the posting (required ones count fully, "nice to have" ones half), each looked up in the profile's experience bullets, projects, skills and certifications. A comparable tool in the same narrow category (Tableau for Power BI, Prefect for Airflow) earns half credit. Every requirement is listed on the application page with where it was found.
Role type20%The posting's title against the role types the profile targets or has held ({{ families|join(", ") }}).
Years asked for20%The largest minimum stated in the required section against the profile's total experience, with overlapping roles counted once.

Role-type cap

When a posting's role type is not one the profile targets or has held (an SDE resume against a Data Analyst posting, say), Fit is capped at {{ cap }}, whoever scored it. The cap is a fixed rule, so an AI score cannot get past it, and it applies before anything automatic reads the score: fast-track review, auto-tailoring and autofill launch. Tailoring a resume afterwards does not lift it. A title the rules don't recognise is never capped.

Without an AI provider

  • Scoring uses the rule-based fit above.
  • Resume tailoring reorders the profile's own bullets and skills so the ones this posting asks for come first. Nothing is added or reworded, so nothing can be invented. No cover letter is written.
  • Outreach drafts are a short template built from facts on record, for you to edit before sending.
  • Interview prep, mock interviews, story drafting, LinkedIn import and the research question box need an AI provider and say so.
